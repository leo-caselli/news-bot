His family announced the news, remembering him as "the greatest jeweller of our time", according to Italian media. Paolo Bulgari was Chairman of the Bvlgari Group since 1984 and its jewellery pieces have adorned Hollywood stars and royalty including Diana, Princess of Wales. The company Bvlgari posted an online tribute to the "true creative soul", whose work would "continue to inspire generations to come". The firm, Bulgari, was set up by his grandfather, Greek silversmith Sotirios Voulgaris, in Rome in the late 19th century - who later adapted his name to Bulgari in Italian. Originally from Paramythia, a mountain village in the Epirus region of northwestern. Greece renowned for its silver jewellery, Voulgaris fled Ottoman rule and settled in Naples before opening a shop in Rome. Paolo Bulgari joined the company shortly after leaving school, becoming an expert in gemstones - eventually leading the business and overseeing international growth. The Bulgari family favoured bold gemstones, rich yellow gold, generous proportions and unexpected colour combinations to set them apart from the delicate asethetic associated with Parisian jewellery houses. The business broke into luxury markets and diversified beyond jewellery into watches, perfumes, leather goods and hotels.
